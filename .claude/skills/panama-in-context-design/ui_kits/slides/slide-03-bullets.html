Why It Matters

What made Panamá Viejo fall

  • Strategic wealth. The city was the Pacific terminus of Spain's silver route — a prime target for raiders.
  • The 1671 raid. The privateer Henry Morgan marched overland and overwhelmed the city's defenses.
  • Fire and flight. The capital burned; survivors carried its stones to a new, fortified site.
  • A second city. In 1673, Casco Antiguo rose on a defensible peninsula two miles west.
