1.3 · The Fall

Morgan's raid, 1671

By the 1670s, Panamá was famous across Europe for its riches. That reputation made it a prize for Henry Morgan, a Welsh privateer operating with the quiet approval of the English crown.

Rather than attack the fortified Caribbean ports directly, Morgan led some 1,400 men across the isthmus on foot. After a difficult jungle march, his force met the city's hastily assembled defenders on the plain outside the walls — and overwhelmed them.

In the chaos that followed, fire swept through the wooden buildings and much of the city was destroyed. Its residents chose not to rebuild on the same exposed site, and two years later founded a new, walled capital to the west.

Above: The bell tower of Panamá Viejo's cathedral still stands among the excavated ruins — the most recognizable survivor of the 1671 destruction.
