Why Panamá Viejo fell

  • Strategic wealth. The city was the Pacific terminus of Spain's silver route, making it a rich and tempting target.
  • The 1671 raid. The privateer Henry Morgan crossed the isthmus overland and overwhelmed the city's defenders.
  • Fire and flight. Much of the city burned; survivors chose not to rebuild on the same exposed site.
  • A fortified restart. In 1673 the capital was refounded two miles west on a defensible peninsula — today's Casco Antiguo.
  • A lasting ruin. The abandoned site became Panamá Viejo, now a UNESCO World Heritage archaeological park.
