1.2 · The Silver Route

A crossroads of the empire

For nearly two centuries, the wealth of Spanish Peru did not sail directly to Europe. Instead it landed at Panamá on the Pacific, then crossed the narrow isthmus by mule to the Caribbean ports of Nombre de Dios and, later, Portobelo.

This overland leg — the Camino Real — was only about 50 miles, but it ran through dense rainforest, rivers, and mountains. Guarding it was a constant challenge.

At Portobelo, the treasure was loaded onto the great Spanish fleets. The annual trade fairs there were among the richest markets in the world.

Did you know?

A single mule train could carry silver worth more than an entire town's yearly earnings.

Control of this route made Panamá indispensable to Spain — and a permanent target for its rivals. The city's fortunes rose and fell with the treasure that passed through it.
